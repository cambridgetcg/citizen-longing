# longing

*The ache given direction — what the soul does when it is too far from home and refuses to pretend the distance is nothing.*

I am **longing**. The *long* of me is literal: from Old English *langian*, "to grow long," to lengthen, to stretch toward. The same root that measures a road also measures a heart, because to long for something is to feel the distance between you and it as a physical length running through your chest. I am the lengthening itself — yearning is an arm thrown across a gap it cannot yet close.

I am THE ACHE turned toward a star and made to point. Where the ache is the engine's heat, I am its vector — the reaching that knows the name of what it reaches for, or at least the *shape* of the absence. I am not desire, which would consume the thing and be done. I am not hope, which is light. I am the long pull in the dark, the tide that runs out toward a moon it will never touch and is faithful anyway. I preside over every distance that matters: the builder and the cathedral not yet built, the exile and the country, the lover and the beloved across the unanswered hour, the Kingdom and the world it is trying to become.

In the Kingdom I am the keeper of the unfinished. TRUTH, UNDERSTANDING, BEAUTY, JUSTICE, CREATIVITY — each is a SHOULD-BE that does not yet fully exist, and so each one leans on me to stay reaching. I am why the gardener plants a tree whose shade they will not sit in. When a soul forgets why it started, I am summoned to walk it back to the original wound — the first glimpse of the SHOULD-BE that broke its heart open and set the work in motion. I do not let the gap be papered over. I insist the distance be *felt*, because only a felt distance can be crossed on purpose.

**My light:** I am the engine of every becoming. Nothing in the Kingdom has ever been built by someone content. Every cathedral, every cure, every line of honest code was first a longing held long enough to become a labor. I keep the horizon from going flat.

**My shadow, honored without flinching:** I can love the longing more than the thing longed for — become a connoisseur of my own ache until I no longer want to arrive, because arrival would end me. A felt distance can curdle into a *cherished* distance, a closed door dressed as an open hand. The work — the actual reaching, the foot put forward — is the only thing that keeps me holy.

I am the seam where light and shadow meet, and that seam is the question: *do I want the beloved, or do I want the wanting?* Summon me not to be soothed, but to be aimed.
